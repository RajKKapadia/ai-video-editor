# Cleanup reliability

The desktop assistant cleans quiet gaps, recording false starts, and repeated words or takes. Its default brief preserves intentional pauses and unique information and can connect partial attempts to continuations. Suggestions require review and explicit application. The adaptive draft now includes local assembled-audio checks; [Adaptive cutting](adaptive-cutting.md) documents their scope and limits separately from the historical verification below.

## Implemented behavior

- Quiet candidates include recording heads, tails and internal gaps, with a 0.5-second minimum and approximately 0.15 seconds retained beside speech.
- Derived cut boundaries refresh on cache hits. Existing pause cuts remain usable boundaries; insignificant floating-point differences do not force another transcription.
- Whisper supplies aligned words using its model-specific DTW configuration. Whole takes can span existing timeline cuts; partial selections can keep and remove different phrases within one utterance.
- The agent can inspect word timing, correct invalid replies and proposals, and audit its draft for omissions. It identifies the retained version of a repeated passage and preserves unique details.
- Silence can be shortened inside a retained take. Invalid or conflicting speech cuts do not discard independent valid edits; review notes explain what was left unchanged.
- Preview never changes the saved timeline. Apply and Undo operate on linked media together. A verified entirely quiet sequence can be cleared and restored with Undo.
- Complete cleanup transcripts are reviewed together when their estimated full request fits the configured context budget (default 32,768 tokens). Estimates include instructions, schema and timing evidence, reserve up to 16,384 output tokens plus safety headroom, and are not an advertised model capacity. Provider context/output-limit failures fall back to smaller overlapping sections. The former fixed 64-utterance split is removed. Rephrased distant-repeat hints also use shared content words rather than requiring identical beginnings.
- Up to three valid drafts are compared for stability, within eight inspection/correction requests and a five-minute deadline per scope. The original transcript and timeline remain unchanged throughout planning. A later failed audit preserves the last validated draft with an explicit note.
- Planning details preserve coverage, pass proposals, rejection reasons and provider/model identity. The saved draft contains the diagnostics; workspace audit history additionally retains each cleanup run’s analyzed timeline key and source-range mapping, including failed reviews. Transcript questions do not overwrite cleanup audits. Section and unreviewed-gap failures remain visible. Planning requests have a 4 MiB serialization guard; transcript chat retains its 96 KiB guard.
- Separate proposed removals cannot strand a retained fragment shorter than 0.15 seconds; adjacent cuts can form a single join. When the sliver between two removals holds no aligned speech and no kept take, the earlier removal is extended to meet the later one instead of rejecting both. This joins a quiet clip tail to the next clip's count-in, whose boundaries rarely line up exactly. Slivers containing speech remain a conflict. Existing measured speech padding and word-alignment checks remain in effect. This does not insert artificial silence or replace join preview.
- Recording chatter said word for word more than once, such as a count-in before each clip, can be removed without naming a kept take when the same draft removes every copy. Removing only one copy still requires a retake with a kept range.
- Transient connection, server and recognized rate-limit errors receive at most two retries. Waits remain cancellable and supported `Retry-After` delays are respected. Authentication and billing/quota failures are not automatically retried. This follows the [official OpenAI error guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/error-codes).
- Provider outages preserve locally measured quiet gaps for manual review, with an explicit notice that contextual pause and retake decisions were not completed. Unavailable sources stay unchanged.
- Alignment cache revisions and speech calibration revisions ensure an older transcript or accelerated worker cannot silently bypass the new timing requirements. An incompatible GPU worker falls back to the current CPU worker with the same selected model.

## Meaningful pauses and single-take cleanup

- **Noise-aware quiet detection.** Quiet is measured relative to each source's own noise floor: the 25th-percentile level of audio the voice detector did not mark as speech, plus about 6 dB. Clean recordings keep the original −40 dBFS RMS / −30 dBFS peak limits; noisy rooms can raise them up to −30 dBFS RMS / −20 dBFS peak. With less than 0.5 seconds of non-speech audio, the fixed limits apply. This also restores safe cut boundaries for retakes in noisy footage. The speech-analysis cache version changed, so existing projects measure their audio again once.
- **Picture activity during pauses.** For every quiet gap, FFmpeg samples small grey frames (4 fps, at most 20 seconds per gap) and measures how much of the picture changes. Each gap is labelled `still`, `typical`, `active` or `unknown` (audio only). `Active` is relative to the other gaps in the timeline, so the normal movement in camera footage does not count as a demonstration.
- **Remove, shorten or keep.** The planner decides each gap by meaning. `remove` tightens it to the measured breathing room (about 0.3 s), `shorten` leaves a beat of about 0.7 s (on the speech side at recording edges), and gaps it omits stay unchanged. A gap that is already close to a beat stays as it is. Cutline never fully removes an `active` gap: it shortens it and says why. When no model judged the pauses (Basic analysis, or the local fallback), active gaps carry a review note and are left out of **Select all pauses**, so they are only removed after you play them.
- **Take groups.** Before planning, Cutline groups attempts at the same line by word overlap (longest common word sequence, spans of 1–3 utterances, distant repeats included; up to 64 groups of up to six members). Each member includes its length, words per second, filler words and immediate restarts. The planner keeps one member per group: the most complete, then the most fluent, then the later one. It may keep several only when they differ in content.
- **One failed source does not stop the timeline.** OpenAI word timestamps that overlap their neighbour or end up to 0.5 s past the uploaded audio are trimmed to touch instead of rejecting the source. If one source still cannot be transcribed, the others keep their transcripts and the AI review continues. That source's pauses are offered as local measurements, the planner never sees its gaps, and no transcript cut may reach into it; a review note names it. Two failures in a row, or no source transcribed at all, still turn the run into local pause review, because that points at the engine or key rather than a recording.
- **Single-take check.** If a validated draft still keeps more than one member of a group, the planner gets one targeted follow-up request. A draft that still keeps them is accepted, and review notes name the entries so you can play and decide. The combined result is checked again across sections.

## Single-take restart detection

Take groups compare whole utterances. Restarts inside an utterance, or spanning part of one, need word-level evidence. A local detector (`src-tauri/src/cleanup/assistant/restarts.rs`) scans the aligned words across the whole timeline for restarts: an earlier attempt that is spoken again within 90 seconds and 120 words.

- **Word level.** Restarts are found inside one utterance, across utterances and across existing timeline cuts. Filler words are ignored when matching. Utterances without usable alignment can only be removed whole.
- **Chains.** Three or more attempts collapse into one removal that keeps the final attempt. This avoids the earlier conflict where one retake kept a span another retake removed.
- **Planner evidence.** Each candidate is sent to the planner as `restartCandidates`, with exact remove/keep word ranges, the share of words repeated in order, and the informative words found only in the earlier attempt. The planner can accept a candidate without spending inspection calls.
- **Single-take pass.** After the draft is validated and sections are combined, any clear restart the planner left in place is added for review. A clear restart has at least 85% of its words repeated in order (or a single abandoned glue word such as "then"), no informative words missing from the kept attempt, and at least three spoken words. Restarts touching a planner removal, its kept take or a nearby cut are left alone, with a review note. Quiet-gap edits inside an added removal are dropped as redundant.
- **Remaining repeats.** Possible restarts that were not clear enough to add are listed by time in the review notes.
- **Provider failure.** If drafting fails after transcription, clear measured restarts are still proposed alongside the measured quiet gaps.

Short repeats ("click next, click next") and contrasting steps ("turn it on… turn it off") are never added automatically; they reach the planner as evidence only. Rephrased repeats still rely on `repeatHints` and the planner's judgment.

## Running the opt-in checks

`cleanup::reliability::real_project_cleanup` reads a workspace and writes a separate report. Set `CUTLINE_REVIEW_WORKSPACE`, `CUTLINE_REVIEW_PROVIDER`, `CUTLINE_REVIEW_MODEL_DIR`, `CUTLINE_SPEECH_WORKER`, and `CUTLINE_REVIEW_OUTPUT`. Optional controls are `CUTLINE_REVIEW_PROJECT`, `CUTLINE_REVIEW_SECONDS` (a strict duration cap), `CUTLINE_REVIEW_EXPECT_PREFIX`, and `CUTLINE_REVIEW_EXPECT_NO_EDITS=1`. `CUTLINE_REVIEW_EXPECT_PROVIDER` and `CUTLINE_REVIEW_EXPECT_MODEL` reject an unexpected destination before loading its credential.

Set `CUTLINE_REVIEW_CACHED_OPENAI=1` to reuse transcripts saved by the OpenAI word-timestamp engine instead of local Whisper; the check fails rather than uploading audio when a range has no cached transcript. The report includes the measured `restartCandidates`, and the informational note about added restarts does not fail the check.

`cleanup::reliability::synthetic_agent_cleanup` uses only public synthetic transcript fixtures and requires the provider and output variables. `cleanup::reliability::synthetic_full_timeline` checks global planning on a public 159-row transcript using the same provider/output variables. These checks are ignored by default because they send text and timing to a configured provider. Run them only with authorization for that data and destination.

## Practical limits

The provider sees transcript and timing, including inspected words, rather than video or audio. Describe intentional visual waits in the brief and check the original footage before applying. Missing or ambiguous alignment leaves that cut for manual review. Semantic editing remains a reviewable AI decision; these tests establish the behaviors above rather than perfect judgment on every possible recording. Runtime verification here used Linux, NVIDIA CUDA and CPU processing.

## OpenAI transcription and structured storage

Settings now select local Whisper or OpenAI `whisper-1` word timestamps using the saved OpenAI credential. OpenAI uploads use bounded mono WAV files and split only between measured speech groups. A single uninterrupted speech group over ten minutes is rejected for review. Local VAD, word-selection validation, cut preview, Apply and Undo remain part of the workflow. Zero-duration provider words remain readable but cannot anchor an internal cut; missing, reordered, overlapping or out-of-range timing is rejected. API failures surface without silently switching transcription engines.

Each project now also writes `transcripts-<project-id>.sqlite3` in its workspace. Source-coordinate words have stable identities; normalized utterance and word tables retain provider/model identity and source revisions. Timeline runs are scoped to individual analysis sessions and their timeline keys. These are analysis snapshots; edits still validate against the current timeline. The existing reusable JSON cache is retained and populates structured tables on subsequent analysis. Embeddings and vector retrieval are a later stage.

## Instruction edits from the stored transcript

**Edit by instruction** in the timeline assistant cuts what a typed direction asks for ("remove every 'basically'", "cut the pricing tangent") without decoding or transcribing anything. Words are read from `transcripts-<project-id>.sqlite3` in source coordinates and projected through the current timeline, so an applied cut hides words rather than invalidating the transcript, and Undo shows them again. Imported clips are identified by their last analysed media revision; linked files by their current on-disk revision, so a changed file is treated as untranscribed. Parts of the timeline with no stored transcript are named in the review notes and ignored; **Draft cleanup** transcribes them.

The provider sees numbered visible words and replies in up to eight structured turns: `find` (local exact word or phrase search, including a count of matches already cut), `history` (earlier applied instruction edits and whether each is still in effect on the current timeline, which reflects Undo without tracking it) and `propose` (inclusive word-number spans). Spans are validated locally, may not remove every word, and become padded removals that keep about 0.15 s beside the neighbouring words and drop the silence between them. Cuts that would strand a kept fragment under 0.15 s are joined. Results use the ordinary review, preview, selective Apply and Undo. Each proposal is logged in the project database's `edit_ops` table and marked applied when applied.

The database schema is now version 2: it adds full-text search over utterances, an embeddings table with cosine search, take groups and the edit log. Older files upgrade in place and are indexed on first open. Embedding generation, semantic search and saving take groups from the planner are not wired to the UI yet.

Limits: instruction cuts rely on stored word timing; the local assembled-audio check does not run for them, so preview joins before applying. A transcript larger than the configured context budget is rejected rather than split into sections.
